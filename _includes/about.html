I originally launched this site as a place for programming tutorials, tool recommendations, and the kinds of written notes I wished I had found more often while learning on my own.

That is no longer the center of gravity. AI agents can already explain syntax, scaffold apps, summarize docs, and walk through many implementation details on demand. The harder and more interesting questions now are about judgment: what to build, what to trust, which dependencies to introduce, how to review agent output, and how teams should adapt their workflows without losing rigor.

So this site is shifting away from tutorial-first writing and more toward musings, product notes, experiments, and practical insights about programming in the age of AI agents. You should expect more writing about developer tooling, workflow design, package selection, security tradeoffs, and what still seems worth learning deeply even as agents get better.

I still care about useful, concrete writing. I just think the highest-value writing now is less "click here, type this" and more "here is how I think about the problem, the constraints, and the tradeoffs."

Happy reading!
